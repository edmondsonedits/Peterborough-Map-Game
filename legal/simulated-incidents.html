Simulation disclosure

Simulated Incident Notice

Emergency Games uses real streets, addresses, businesses, institutions, parks, landmarks, and other recognizable places because geographic familiarity is part of the training experience.

All incidents shown by the simulator are fictional training scenarios. A business, residence, organization, or landmark appearing in a dispatch does not mean that the displayed incident actually happened there.

Why real place names are used

Real place names can help users learn street locations, major destinations, access points, intersections, and recognizable landmarks. Their inclusion is for geographic identification and training context.

No affiliation or endorsement is implied

References to businesses, organizations, public facilities, municipalities, fire services, paramedic services, hospitals, or other real-world entities do not imply sponsorship, endorsement, partnership, approval, or participation unless expressly stated for a specific deployment.

Sensitive scenarios

Medical emergencies, fires, alarms, rescues, overdoses, wellness checks, hazardous conditions, and other incident types are generated or selected for simulation. They should not be interpreted as statements about a real business, property, occupant, employee, customer, patient, or historical event.

Geographic descriptions may use nearby landmarks

Where useful for training, a scenario may describe an incident as being outside, near, beside, behind, or in the area of a recognizable business or landmark. This describes the simulated location only and does not attribute the fictional incident to that business or organization.
